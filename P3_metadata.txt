TITLE:
In-Context Binding Capacity in Language Models: What the Query and Readout Change

AUTHORS:
Manas Venkata Sai Ravulapalli; Samrath Chadha

AFFILIATION:
Efficient Computation Inc.

FIGURES: 5

ABSTRACT:
Language models must retrieve associations specified by their context to answer questions about them. We study how recall changes as more name-value pairs are supplied to 16 open models from 70M to 2.8B parameters. The interpretation of the resulting capacity curves depends on how the model is queried and how its answer is read. At sixteen bindings, deleting is from the question raises Pythia-410M recall from 0.226 to 0.896 on the same assignments, without changing the weights or scoring rule. We then hold the original question fixed and compare supervised hidden-state probes with trained candidate-logit decoders. In a preregistered test, the probes exceed the candidate-logit decoders in all three held-out models; two also meet the registered ten-point advantage over native output. These results show that poor recall under one question is insufficient to establish a model's binding limit.
