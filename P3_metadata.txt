TITLE:
In-Context Binding Capacity in Language Models: What the Query and Readout Change

AUTHORS:
Manas Venkata Sai Ravulapalli; Samrath Chadha

AFFILIATION:
Efficient Computation Inc.

FIGURES: 5

ABSTRACT:
Supplying task-specific information in a prompt offers a way to adapt a language model without retraining it. The usefulness of this approach depends on reliably retrieving the association needed for the current question, especially when model size is constrained. Before treating poor recall as evidence that a larger model is needed, we ask how much the failure depends on the question and readout. We study this issue as more name-value pairs are supplied to 16 open models from 70M to 2.8B parameters. At sixteen bindings, deleting is from the question raises Pythia-410M recall from 0.226 to 0.896 on the same assignments, without changing the weights or scoring rule. With the original question unchanged, we also train classifiers to predict the assigned value from either the model's hidden states or its scores for candidate values. We specify this test before fitting the classifiers on three additional models. At sixteen bindings, three-quarter depth and no distractors, the hidden-state classifiers predict more values correctly than the classifiers using candidate-value scores across all test trials in each additional model. Two also meet the registered ten-percentage-point advantage over choosing the highest-scoring candidate value. How much a model appears to retrieve therefore depends on how it is asked and how its answer is obtained.
