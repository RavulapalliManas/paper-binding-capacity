TITLE:
In-Context Binding Capacity in Language Models: What the Query and Readout Change

AUTHORS:
Manas Venkata Sai Ravulapalli; Samrath Chadha

AFFILIATION:
Efficient Computation Inc.

FIGURES: 5

ABSTRACT:
Supplying task-specific information in a prompt offers a way to adapt a language model without retraining it. The usefulness of this approach depends on reliably retrieving the association needed for the current question, especially when model size is constrained. Before treating poor recall as evidence that a larger model is needed, we ask how much the failure depends on the question and readout. We study this issue as more name-value pairs are supplied to 16 open models from 70M to 2.8B parameters. At sixteen bindings, deleting is from the question raises Pythia-410M recall from 0.226 to 0.896 on the same assignments, without changing the weights or scoring rule. A preregistered test holds the original question fixed and compares classifiers trained on hidden activations with classifiers trained on candidate-value logits. At sixteen bindings, three-quarter depth and no distractors, the hidden-state classifiers achieve higher accuracy over all test trials in all three held-out models. Two also meet the registered ten-percentage-point advantage over choosing the highest-scoring candidate value. The results show that an apparent retrieval limit can change substantially with the procedure used to query the model and read its states.
