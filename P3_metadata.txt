TITLE:
In-Context Binding Capacity in Language Models: What the Query and Readout Change

AUTHORS:
Manas Venkata Sai Ravulapalli; Samrath Chadha

AFFILIATION:
Efficient Computation Inc.

FIGURES: 5

ABSTRACT:
Supplying information in a prompt lets a language model adapt to a task without retraining. When retrieval deteriorates as more associations enter the context, what does that decline tell us about the model's capacity? A low score leaves open whether another question would elicit the answer or whether a trained classifier could predict it more accurately from the model's internal state. This distinction matters when model size is constrained: poor recall alone does not tell us whether a larger model is needed. We measure recall as more name-value pairs are supplied to 16 open models from 70M to 2.8B parameters. In subsets of these models, we compare questions on matched assignments and supervised predictions from hidden states and candidate-value scores. At sixteen bindings, deleting is from the question raises Pythia-410M recall from 0.226 to 0.896 on the same assignments, without changing the weights or scoring rule. With the original question unchanged, we also train classifiers to predict the assigned value from either the model's hidden states or its scores for candidate values. We specify this test before fitting the classifiers on three additional models. At sixteen bindings, three-quarter depth and no distractors, the hidden-state classifiers predict more values correctly than the classifiers using candidate-value scores across all test trials in each additional model. Two also meet the registered ten-percentage-point advantage over choosing the highest-scoring candidate value. These comparisons connect failures under load to the question and prediction method, limiting what low recall can establish about model capacity.
